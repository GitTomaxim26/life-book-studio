# Things That May Deserve the Charter

*A holding pen. These are promising principles discovered during building — but the Charter
earned its authority by admitting ideas only after they survived sustained contact with reality,
not first contact. Ideas live here until they've proven durable through actual use. Promotion to
the Charter is deliberate and rare.*

---

## Candidate 1 — Manners before senses
*Discovered: building the Sandbox Muse (the honest question-only conversation loop).*

The Muse's **behavior** (notice, quote, point, ask — never conclude) is independent of its
**intelligence**. A Muse that cannot yet read the book can still behave correctly: it can offer
a question worth sitting with without interpreting, claiming, or pretending to understand. The
sandbox proved this felt *alive* and *honest* at the same time, with no model behind it.

*Implication if it holds:* the right behaviors can — and should — be built and felt before the
intelligence arrives, so that when intelligence comes, it inherits manners that are already correct.

*Not yet promoted because:* felt true in one session. Needs to survive extended use — especially
the test of whether it still feels honest (not stiff or mechanical) over many interactions.

---

## Candidate 2 — A well-offered question is already the product
*Discovered: same session — the realization on seeing the sandbox move.*

The value of the Muse may come primarily from the **quality of the question offered**, not from
**personalization or intelligence**. A beautifully presented, genuinely good question is valuable
whether or not the Muse has read your book. Intelligence *aims* a question at the right page; it
does not make a mediocre question good.

*Implication if it holds:* the build path is question-library-first
(sandbox → better questions → context-aware → book-aware → generative intelligence *much later*),
not a rush to AI. Aligns with the Charter's "restraint is the product."

*Not yet promoted because:* it's a strategic hypothesis from one set of screenshots. Needs to be
tested by whether people actually find static, well-chosen questions worth returning to over time.
