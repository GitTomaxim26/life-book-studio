# Editions and the Time Axis

*Status: captured, not built. The most important governance note about what Life Book is
**across a lifetime** — the one thing the design had never pinned down. Discovered in
dialogue (prompted by studying LUMARA's "CHRONICLE"). Like all candidates: reasoned, not
yet lived. Do not build until at least one edition has been written and finished — finishing
the first edition is what will teach what beginning a second one should feel like.*

---

## The resolved question

**Life Book is Many Editions, not One Living Document.**

- Not: one perpetual document you edit forever, latest version = truth, old versions = history.
  (That is software wearing a book's clothes. It can never be *finished*, so it can never be
  *held*. It becomes a Notion page.)
- But: **Life Book 2026, Life Book 2031, Life Book 2038** — each a complete, true, preserved
  artifact. You never overwrite. You begin a new edition.

**Why this is the truest form, not a compromise:** a book's dignity comes from being a
*finished object* — an heirloom, not a feed. And crucially: **an old edition is never wrong.**
Life Book 2026 is not an outdated draft of 2031 — it is who you were in 2026, completely and
truthfully. You don't overwrite it because it was never false. This is the opposite of software
(where old versions are bugs to patch); here, old editions are *true forever.*

## The hierarchy

Not the journal's hierarchy (`Entry → Month → Year → Narrative`).
The book's hierarchy: **`Book → Edition → Era → Lifetime`.**

## The unit is transformation, not time

The meaningful unit of a life is the *shift*, not the day:
"I became a father," "I moved countries," "I stopped drinking," "I started a company."
The app is *already* built on this — its whole spine is a transformation arc
(Identity now → the turning point → New Identity → the future). A new edition is triggered by
*becoming someone new*, never by the calendar.

## The non-negotiable: the AUTHOR decides when an edition is complete

Not the calendar. Not the AI. Not the system. **The author.** This is the same authorship
principle that governs everything else, surfacing at the largest scale:
- The Muse proposes a thread → the author *accepts* it.
- The Muse never names who you're becoming → the author *writes* New Identity.
- The Muse never declares you've changed → the author *decides* a volume is complete.

The pen stays in the same hand from the scale of a sentence to the scale of a lifetime. A
principle that holds at every scale is the *grain* of the thing, not a rule imposed on it.

The felt trigger (not a metric): **"You no longer recognize yourself as the author of
Chapter One."** A transformation trigger — almost impossible to fake. Not word count, not a
date, not "the AI thinks you've changed."

## Practically: how a volume reaches the shelf

**"Finish This Edition"** — a deliberate, near-ceremonial act. Author-pressed, never inferred,
never suggested, never automatic. When pressed:
- the edition becomes immutable / archived,
- it gets a date and edition number,
- it can be generated as a PDF / printed book,
- it goes onto the shelf.

Then life happens — months, years. If and when the author feels they've become someone
different: **"Begin New Edition"** (Volume II). Author-initiated. Not because the system
detected a transformation — because the author *knows*.

## Finishing is the SUCCESS state

Most products fear the user finishing (= churn) and engineer infinite engagement. Life Book
should *want* you to finish. A completed edition is success, not failure. The product respects
your life enough to let you *leave it* — and trusts that life itself (a real transformation)
will bring you back for Volume II. The retention mechanism is not a streak or a notification;
it is *becoming a different person.* Only a product with a soul instead of a funnel can offer that.

## Important correction: witnessing is NOT journaling (do not conflate)

CHRONICLE-as-journaling — daily entries → monthly summaries → yearly summaries — **is rejected.**
That is logging time; it would turn Life Book into one of a hundred journaling apps and dissolve
its identity.

But **witnessing is NOT rejected, and may be one of the most powerful things the product can do.**
Witnessing = seeing *Identity (2026)*, *Identity (2029)*, *Identity (2034)* **side by side** —
the author seeing themselves change across editions. That is not a feed; it is a mirror held
across years. Journaling logs time; witnessing reveals transformation. These are different things.
Keep the mirror; reject the feed.

## What this rejects vs keeps (summary)

- REJECT: one living editable document; calendar/AI/system-decided completion; daily→monthly→yearly
  journaling timeline; infinite-engagement retention.
- KEEP: many finished editions; author-decided, transformation-triggered, ceremonial completion;
  witnessing (versions side by side); finishing-as-success; the pen always in the author's hand.

*Not yet promoted / not yet built because:* no edition has been finished yet. The first edition
must be lived and completed before the architecture of a second one is designed. Reasoned, not lived.
